# P11 — MTB M1 Knowledge, Suspension Learning & MTB Workshop Expansion

## Objective

Promote MTB M1 from P10's architectural second-bike foundation into a genuinely useful second Bike Atlas content family.

P11 makes Learn, Workshop and Encyclopedia bike-owned instead of global Road-only catalogs, then fills MTB M1 with its own technical content.

## Bike-owned content architecture

Lessons and Workshop procedures now declare a stable `bikeId`.

The shared catalogs expose:

- `getLessonsForBike(bikeId)`;
- `getWorkshopProceduresForBike(bikeId)`;
- global lookup by stable lesson/procedure ID for deep links.

Progress remains device-local and shared in the existing stores because lesson and procedure IDs are globally unique.

The viewer restores the owning bike whenever a lesson or procedure is opened.

## MTB Learn

P11 adds four MTB-specific interactive lessons.

### 1. How mountain-bike suspension works

Covers:

- front suspension travel;
- rear shock + linkage interaction;
- sag as a measured setup concept;
- compression vs rebound damping;
- identifying the suspension linkage.

No universal sag percentage or damping setting is asserted.

### 2. Why mountain bikes use dropper posts

Covers:

- extended vs lowered saddle positions;
- remote actuation;
- body-movement clearance;
- identifying the dropper post.

### 3. How a 1× mountain-bike drivetrain works

Covers:

- one front chainring;
- wide-range rear cassette;
- rear-only shifting;
- chain management on rough terrain;
- identifying the cassette.

### 4. Trail tires, grip and pressure

Covers:

- contact patch;
- tread roles;
- casing/rim support;
- tire-pressure trade-offs.

The lesson explicitly rejects a universal MTB pressure value.

## MTB Workshop

P11 adds four safe generic MTB procedures.

### Suspension pre-ride inspection

Checks:

- clean stanchion/shock surfaces;
- obvious fork damage/leakage;
- rear-shock condition;
- linkage/pivot play or abnormal movement.

### Suspension sag baseline

Teaches a repeatable measurement process:

- record current settings;
- consult exact component/frame guidance;
- establish a manufacturer-guided starting pressure;
- assume a repeatable rider position;
- measure sag;
- compare against the correct manufacturer range;
- adjust in small increments within published limits.

Bike Atlas does not invent pressure or sag targets.

### Dropper-post function check

Checks:

- visible condition;
- smooth travel;
- remote response;
- position holding under normal controlled load.

No internal dropper disassembly is taught.

### Trail-tire pre-ride check

Checks:

- tread/casing damage;
- measured pressure;
- actual tire/rim pressure limits;
- bead seating and rim condition;
- rotating-wheel behavior.

Again, no universal pressure number is supplied.

## Cross-bike runtime

Entering an MTB lesson/procedure keeps `MTB M1` rendered.

Entering Road content keeps `Road R1` rendered.

Bike-aware content URLs use the owning bike:

```text
/?bike=mtb-m1&lesson=mtb-suspension-basics
/?bike=mtb-m1&lesson=mtb-dropper-basics
/?bike=mtb-m1&workshop=mtb-sag-baseline
/?bike=mtb-m1&workshop=mtb-trail-tire-check
```

The owning bike is also inferred from the lesson/procedure ID if the `bike` parameter is absent.

## Assembly boundary

Workshop operation lookup is now bike-aware.

MTB M1 still has zero authored removal/disassembly operations. P11's MTB procedures are setup and inspection workflows, so they do not pretend that the P10 structural assembly graph already encodes service sequences.

## Validation

P11 validates:

- exactly one MTB encyclopedia profile per semantic component;
- profile fields and relationships;
- lesson bike ownership;
- lesson focus/highlight/challenge references;
- interactive challenge targets;
- Workshop bike ownership;
- tools and prerequisite procedure references;
- interactive Workshop focus targets;
- operation references when present.

Road validation also checks that Road lessons and Workshop procedures remain owned by Road R1.
