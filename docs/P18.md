# P18 — Bicycle History, Evolution Timeline & Engineering Lineage

## Objective

Add history to Bike Atlas as an engineering system, not as a detached chronological article.

P18 answers two questions for each historical milestone:

1. **What changed mechanically?**
2. **Where does that idea survive in a modern Bike Atlas bicycle?**

The result is a sourced timeline that can jump directly from a historical innovation into the current 3D component model.

## History model

P18 adds:

```text
engine/history/types.ts
content/history/timeline.json
domain/history/catalog.ts
components/history/HistoryPanel.tsx
scripts/validate-history.mjs
```

Each milestone contains:

- stable event ID;
- year or period;
- date-confidence type;
- title / subtitle / summary;
- engineering shifts;
- category tags;
- modern Bike Atlas lineage targets;
- historical caveat when attribution or dating is not simple;
- one or more external sources.

## Eras

The timeline is divided into six navigable eras:

```text
1817–1862   Origins
1863–1884   Mechanical bicycle
1885–1901   Modern layout
1902–1969   Gearing & utility
1970–1999   Off-road evolution
2000–today  Modern specialization
```

These era boundaries are navigation structure for Bike Atlas, not claims that bicycle history changed instantly on those dates.

## Milestones

P18 currently contains twelve milestones.

### 1817 — The steerable two-wheeler

Karl Drais's running machine establishes the steerable in-line two-wheel architecture.

Modern lineage links include:

- forks;
- steering;
- headsets.

### c. 1863 — Pedal velocipede

Pedals are added to the front axle in the Michaux workshop.

The timeline explicitly preserves the Smithsonian's attribution warning: the credit cannot be assigned with certainty between Pierre Michaux and Pierre Lallement.

### 1871 — The high-wheel search for speed

James Starley's Ariel represents the wire-spoked Ordinary era.

The event explains why increasing the directly driven front-wheel diameter increased distance per pedal revolution, and why the architecture created severe forward-crash risk.

### 1880s — Safety bicycle convergence

Equal-sized wheels, chain drive and gearing converge toward the recognizable modern bicycle layout.

The event is deliberately a **period**, not a single-inventor timestamp.

### 1888 — Practical pneumatic bicycle tire

John Boyd Dunlop develops the practical pneumatic bicycle tire that helped transform bicycle comfort and rolling behavior.

P18 also preserves the Robert William Thomson antecedent from the 1840s.

### 1890s — Bicycle as everyday transportation

Safety-bicycle architecture, pneumatic tires and improving brakes support a major mobility boom.

This event connects historically into the Urban U1 utility lineage.

### 1902 — Internal-gear hub

The first Sturmey-Archer three-speed gear hub provides a clear historical branch toward modern enclosed utility gearing.

Modern lineage:

```text
Urban U1
├─ internal-gear rear hub
├─ hub shifter
└─ single rear sprocket
```

### 1937 — Derailleur adoption in the Tour de France

P18 uses 1937 as a milestone in derailleur **acceptance**, not invention.

The event explicitly states that derailleur mechanisms existed earlier.

Modern lineage reaches:

- Road R1;
- MTB M1;
- Gravel G1.

### 1970s — Mountain-bike emergence

P18 rejects a one-person / one-place invention story.

The timeline describes mountain biking as an enthusiast-driven evolution in multiple communities, while representing Marin County's Repack scene as a well-documented focal point for repeated experimentation.

### 1976 — First Repack time trial

The repeated timed descent becomes an engineering feedback loop for braking, control, durability and purpose-built off-road equipment.

### 1990–1996 — Mountain biking becomes a global discipline

The first UCI Mountain Bike World Championships were held in 1990 and mountain bike entered the Olympic programme in 1996.

This connects into increasingly specialized suspension, frame and trail systems.

### Today — Specialized branches

The final milestone is explicitly marked as a Bike Atlas engineering interpretation.

It links the common safety-bicycle ancestry into:

```text
Road R1
MTB M1
Urban U1
Gravel G1
```

and explains that specialization now comes from geometry, tires, gearing, controls, suspension, accessories and intended environment.

## Trace into Bike Atlas

The core P18 interaction is **Trace it into Bike Atlas**.

Example:

```text
1888 pneumatic tire
       ↓
Road R1 front tire
MTB M1 front tire
Urban U1 front tire
Gravel G1 front tire
       ↓
open selected modern component in 3D
```

The transition:

- changes to the correct bike;
- selects the target component;
- closes History;
- returns the viewer to Normal inspection mode;
- updates the URL.

This makes history part of the component knowledge graph rather than a separate reading destination.

## Attribution discipline

P18 avoids several misleading simplifications.

It does not claim:

- a single uncontested inventor of the bicycle;
- a single uncontested inventor of pedal propulsion;
- that Dunlop was the first person ever to conceive a pneumatic tire;
- that the derailleur was invented in 1937;
- that one individual or one location single-handedly invented mountain biking;
- that modern Gravel, Road, MTB and Urban categories all appeared at one exact historical date.

Where the record is gradual or contested, the timeline says so.

## Validation

`validate-history.mjs` checks:

- schema version;
- event/era ID uniqueness;
- chronological ordering;
- era coverage;
- valid category vocabulary;
- meaningful engineering shifts;
- at least one source per milestone;
- HTTPS source URLs;
- valid source metadata;
- every lineage bike ID;
- every lineage component ID;
- required caveats for contested milestones;
- all four modern archetypes in the final specialization event;
- expected evidence-source families;
- History UI routing;
- lineage navigation;
- History deep-link read/write behavior.
