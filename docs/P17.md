## Gravel Learn

P17 adds four interactive lessons.

### 1. Why gravel tires use more volume

Covers:

- tire width and air volume;
- pressure/support tradeoffs;
- surface-dependent pressure;
- tubeless concepts;
- tire/rim compatibility.

The lesson intentionally does **not** assign one PSI value to a 45 mm tire.

It teaches:

```text
usable pressure
= tire
+ rim
+ rider/bike load
+ casing
+ surface
+ setup
```

instead of treating nominal width as a complete pressure prescription.

### 2. How a 1× gravel drivetrain spans mixed terrain

Covers:

```text
crankset
  ↓
single chainring
  ↓
chain
  ↓
wide-range cassette
  ↓
rear derailleur
```

The lesson explains:

- why front shifting disappears;
- how small/large rear sprockets change gearing;
- how the derailleur manages lateral movement and chain length;
- the tradeoff between broad range, ratio steps and system simplicity.

Exact tooth counts and compatibility remain drivetrain-specific.

### 3. Why gravel bikes use flared drop bars

Covers:

- tops / hoods / drops;
- flare and lower-bar width;
- steering leverage;
- integrated controls;
- whole-bike handling.

The lesson explicitly avoids claiming that flare alone defines gravel handling.

Control remains a system involving:

- tires;
- geometry;
- wheelbase;
- brakes;
- rider position;
- handlebar shape.

### 4. Mounts, bags and light bikepacking loads

Covers:

- purpose-built frame bosses;
- fork mounts;
- steering effects of fork-mounted mass;
- downtube protection;
- cargo position and handling.

The lesson reinforces:

> A visible threaded boss does not reveal its allowable load.

Load capacity remains defined by the actual frame/fork, fastener and accessory system.

## Gravel Workshop

P17 adds four guided procedures.

### Mixed-surface pre-ride check

Checks:

- tire condition and real-system pressure;
- wheel rotation;
- braking;
- exposed drivetrain;
- cockpit;
- mounted equipment.

### Tire, rim and tubeless-condition inspection

Checks:

- actual tire/rim compatibility;
- casing condition;
- bead seating;
- rim damage;
- pressure;
- abnormal pressure loss.

It explicitly avoids universal:

- PSI values;
- sealant amounts;
- tape width;
- valve specification;
- sealant maintenance interval.

### 1× drivetrain inspection

Checks:

- chain;
- chainring;
- cassette;
- rear derailleur;
- shift behavior;
- drivetrain-specific service boundary.

Bike Atlas does not recommend bending a derailleur/hanger by eye or applying adjustment instructions from an unrelated drivetrain generation.

### Bikepacking mounts and cargo clearance

Checks:

- mount condition;
- actual fastener and load limits;
- fork/frame compatibility;
- wheel/brake/steering clearance;
- soft-luggage movement;
- controlled loaded handling.

The procedure uses the **lowest applicable documented limit** across the real frame, fork, fastener and accessory system.
